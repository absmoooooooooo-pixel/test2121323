Weight Calculation Formula

W = [H × Tw + 3 × (B − Tw) × Tf] × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · H = Overall height (mm) · B = Overall width (mm) · Tw = Web thickness (mm) · Tf = Flange thickness (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel E Section
  1. Convert H, B, Tw, Tf, and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area of the E section: Area = H×Tw + 3×(B − Tw)×Tf (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: H is overall height, B overall width, Tw web thickness, Tf flange thickness (top, middle, bottom), L length, and ρ density.

Assumptions/limitations: Assumes a solid E-shaped section with a full-height web and three equal-thickness flanges projecting to overall width B. Theoretical sharp-corner geometry; actual profiles may vary.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an E-section and how is its weight calculated?

An E-section is a single web with three parallel flanges on one side, often used for frames, tracks and guides. The area used here is H × Tw + 3 × (B − Tw) × Tf, multiplied by length and density.

What does a 100 × 50 mm E-section weigh with a 6 mm web and 8 mm flange?

Its area is 100 × 6 + 3 × 44 × 8 = 1,656 mm², so it weighs about 13.0 kg per metre in mild steel. A 6 m length is roughly 78 kg.

Why does the formula use 3 × (B − Tw)?

The section has three flanges of width B, and each one overlaps the web by Tw. Taking (B − Tw) for each flange counts the shared strip once, in the web term.

How does an E-section compare with two C channels back to back?

Two C channels joined back to back form four flanges, while an E-section has three. For the same height, width and thicknesses the E-section is therefore lighter, and its centroid is offset toward the flange side.

Can the three flanges have different thicknesses?

The calculator assumes one flange thickness Tf for all three. If your profile is milled with unequal flanges, calculate each flange as B × T and add them to the web area yourself.

Is the root radius between the web and flanges included?

No. It is a sharp-corner model, so a rolled or extruded E-section with visible fillets is a little heavier than the calculated value.

How much does an 8 mm flange instead of 6 mm change the weight?

Flange contribution is 3 × (B − Tw) × Tf, so it scales almost linearly with flange thickness. On a 100 × 50 section with a 6 mm web, going from 6 mm to 8 mm flanges adds about 25% of the flange weight.

Does the quantity field total the mass of several E-sections?

Yes. Set Quantity to the number of pieces; the calculator shows the weight of one piece, the total weight and the total in metric tonnes.

Can I use this for an aluminium E-profile?

Yes. Pick Aluminium or enter a custom density in g/cm³; the geometry stays the same and only the density term changes.
