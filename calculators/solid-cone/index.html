Weight Calculation Formula

V = (1 ÷ 3) × π × r² × h,   r = D ÷ 2,   W = V × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · D = Base diameter (mm) · r = Radius (mm) · h = Height (mm) · V = Volume (mm³) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel Solid Cone
  1. Convert diameter D and height h to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches (1 in = 25.4 mm).
  2. Calculate radius r = D/2.
  3. Calculate cone volume: V = (1/3) × π × r² × h (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg), where ρ is in g/cm³ (e.g. carbon steel 7.85 g/cm³).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: D is base diameter, h is perpendicular height, r is base radius. All dimensions are in mm and density is in g/cm³ (1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³).

Assumptions/limitations: Assumes a completely solid right circular cone with uniform material. Actual weight may vary with tolerances, shape accuracy, and material composition.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of a solid cone?

The volume of a cone is one third of the base area times the height, V = (π ÷ 3) × r² × h. Multiply that volume by the density, or enter the base diameter and height here.

What does a 100 mm base diameter, 150 mm high steel cone weigh?

The base area is 7,854 mm² and the volume 392,699 mm³, so the cone weighs about 3.08 kg in mild steel at 7.85 g/cm³.

Why is a cone one third of a cylinder with the same base and height?

That is the geometric volume relationship for any cone or pyramid: three identical cones fill the matching cylinder. It is why cone weights are much lower than a bar of the same diameter.

How do I weigh a hollow cone or a conical reducer?

Use the truncated cone calculator, which works from the large and small diameters, vertical height and plate thickness. A hollow cone has no single solid volume, so this solid-cone tool would overstate the weight.

Is the diameter the base of the cone or the tip?

It is the base diameter, the widest circle. The tip is a point, so the volume depends entirely on the base size and the height.

How does cone height affect the weight?

Weight is directly proportional to height at a fixed base diameter, because the volume term is (π ÷ 3) × r² × h.

Can I use this for a cast or forged cone?

Yes, for a solid homogeneous cone of the entered density. Castings may include a draft angle, a riser or a cored bore, which change the real mass.

What if the cone has a flat top instead of a point?

That is a truncated cone (frustum). Enter the two end diameters and the height in the truncated cone calculator rather than using this one.

Does the quantity field multiply the single cone weight?

Yes. Set Quantity to the number of pieces and the total weight and metric tonnage update; the single-piece weight stays visible for reference.
