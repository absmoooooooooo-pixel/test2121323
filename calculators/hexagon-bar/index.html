Weight Calculation Formula

W = (√3 ÷ 2) × A² × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · A = Across-flats dimension (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³) · √3 ≈ 1.73205

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel Hexagon Bar
  1. Convert A and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area of a regular hexagon from across-flats: Area = (√3/2) × A² (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: A is the distance across flats of the regular hexagon, L is bar length, and ρ is material density.

Assumptions/limitations: Assumes a solid regular hexagonal bar with uniform across-flats dimension. Actual weight may vary with tolerances and material grade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of a hexagonal bar?

A regular hexagon with across-flats size A has an area of (√3 ÷ 2) × A². Multiply that by the length and the density to get the mass, which is the formula used in this calculator.

What does across-flats (A/F) mean on a hex bar?

Across-flats is the distance between two opposite flat faces, which is how hex bar is ordered and how a spanner grips a nut. It is not the distance between opposite corners, which is larger by a factor of 1.155.

What is the weight of a 40 mm across-flats hex steel bar per metre?

The area is 1,385.6 mm², so the bar weighs about 10.88 kg per metre at 7.85 g/cm³. A 6 m length comes to roughly 65.3 kg.

What does a 20 mm hex bar weigh per metre?

About 2.72 kg/m in mild steel, because the area is (√3 ÷ 2) × 400 = 346.4 mm². This is the value behind the common shortcut A² × 0.0068 for steel.

How much lighter is a hex bar than a square bar of the same size?

A hexagon fills 86.6% of the square that encloses it, so a 40 mm hex bar weighs about 10.88 kg/m against 12.56 kg/m for a 40 mm square bar.

I only know the across-corners size. How do I enter it?

Convert it first: across-flats = 0.866 × across-corners. A bar measuring 46.2 mm across corners is a 40 mm across-flats hexagon.

Can I use this for hex nut or bolt-head blanks?

Yes, the result is the solid hex weight. If the blank will be drilled or bored, subtract the hole separately, or use the hexagonal bar with round hole calculator.

Which units should A and L be in?

Millimetres with the MM button, or inches with the Inches button. In inches the length is also in inches, and the result is still kilograms.

Why is the measured weight slightly below the calculated value?

Hot-rolled hexagon bars are produced to a minus tolerance on the across-flats size and have rounded corners, so they usually weigh marginally less than the nominal sharp-corner figure.
