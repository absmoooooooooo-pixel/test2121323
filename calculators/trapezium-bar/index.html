Weight Calculation Formula

W = ((A + B) ÷ 2) × H × L × ρ ÷ 10⁶

W = Weight (kg) · A, B = Parallel sides (mm) · H = Perpendicular height (mm) · L = Length (mm) · ρ = Density (g/cm³)

How to Calculate (Step-by-Step)

Carbon Steel Trapezium Bar
  1. Convert A, B, H, and L to millimetres (mm) if entered in inches.
  2. Calculate cross-sectional area: Area = ((A + B) / 2) × H (mm²).
  3. Calculate volume: V = Area × L (mm³).
  4. Calculate weight: W = V × ρ ÷ 1,000,000 (kg).
  5. Multiply by quantity for total weight.

Variables: A and B are the lengths of the two parallel sides of the trapezium cross-section, H is the perpendicular distance between them, L is bar length, and ρ is density.

Assumptions/limitations: Assumes a solid bar with uniform trapezium cross-section and H measured perpendicular to the parallel sides. Actual weight may vary with tolerances and material grade.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you calculate the weight of a trapezium bar?

The area of a trapezium is the average of the two parallel sides times the perpendicular height: ((A + B) ÷ 2) × H. Multiply that by length and density for the weight.

What does a 60 mm / 40 mm trapezium bar weigh if it is 30 mm high?

The area is ((60 + 40) ÷ 2) × 30 = 1,500 mm², so a one-metre length weighs about 11.78 kg in mild steel.

What exactly does H mean in this calculator?

H is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel sides, not the length of the sloping side. Using the slant length would overstate the area and the weight.

How is a trapezium bar different from the trapezoidal bar calculator?

Both use the same average-width formula. Trapezium is used here for the isosceles form with two equal sloping sides, while the trapezoidal bar page takes top and bottom widths for any trapezoid, including a right-angled one.

Can I use this for a keystone, gib or wear bar?

Yes, these are common trapezium sections. Measure the two parallel widths and the perpendicular height on the drawing, and the calculator returns the nominal mass.

What happens if the two parallel sides are equal?

The shape becomes a rectangle; the formula collapses to A × H and matches a rectangular bar of the same size. That is a quick way to sanity-check your inputs.

I only know the sloping side length. How do I find H?

For an isosceles trapezium, H = √(side² − ((A − B) ÷ 2)²). For an unequal trapezoid, take the perpendicular height directly from the drawing.

How do I calculate the weight of a 2 m trapezium bar?

Enter 2000 mm as the length, or multiply the per-metre weight by 2. At 11.78 kg/m a 2 m bar is about 23.6 kg.

Why might the delivered bar differ from the calculated weight?

Tapered sections are rolled to tolerances on both widths and on the height, and the corners may be rounded. The calculated value is the nominal mass from the dimensions you enter.
